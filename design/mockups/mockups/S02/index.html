How it works

A screening layer that can prove it ran.

Blindseal is a privacy-preserving, verifiable screening layer for AI agents. It sits between an agent and its tools as a local proxy, inspecting every tool call with four layers of prompt-injection detection before the call executes. Nothing sensitive leaves the machine: secrets and PII are redacted before the judge sees them, and the judge runs locally or inside a TEE whose attestation you can present on demand.

02 / Four layers

Two layers that match, two that judge.

L0 and L1 are deterministic: the same payload always gets the same answer, so the proof can cover them. L2 and L3 are model-based; they run locally or in a TEE and are attested, not proven.

  • L0RulesExact matches, allowlists and denylists over the raw payload: program ids, recipients, tool paths. Proven
  • L1PatternsExpressions for override phrasing, prompt extraction, hidden unicode, role tags and exfiltration links. Proven
  • L2SimilarityScores the redacted payload against known injections, from 0 to 1. Runs locally or in a TEE.Attested
  • L3JudgeReads the redacted payload when L2 or a rule raises a doubt, and can clear an L2 flag. Runs locally or in a TEE.Attested

04 / The attestation

One attestation, one action, one use.

An attestation starts from one screened decision and ends in one Solana instruction. Everything in between happens on the machine.

When something downstream needs proof — an escrow contract, a counterparty, an auditor — Blindseal produces a zero-knowledge attestation that a specific tool call passed a committed policy. The attestation binds to the action itself: target program, recipient, amount, policy root, and expiry are hashed into it, so it is worthless for any other call. It is single-use. The proof is generated locally and verified with one Solana instruction, so any program can gate agent actions without seeing the prompt, the arguments, the operator's rules, or which agent was screened.

06 / The audit chain

A log that commits to itself.

Every decision is written to a hash-chained local log with selective disclosure: prove a time window was clean, or that a specific event occurred, without exposing traffic.

Hash-chained

Change one entry, break every hash after it

Each entry stores the hash of the one before it, its decision id, its payload hash and its verdict. Anyone holding the head can walk the chain back and recompute every link.

    Selective disclosure

    Show a window, not the traffic

    A disclosure is a signed range of entries: the hashes and verdicts from the highlighted window, checkable against the head, with no payload in it.

    07 / What we claim

    A deliberately narrow claim.

    One claim per call, and a plain line where it stops. The left column is what a verifier never learns; the right is all it gets.

    What we claim

    Blindseal proves that a specific call was screened under a committed policy by a specific detector version, and that the deterministic layers passed. The model-based layers run locally or in a TEE and are attested, not proven. Blindseal does not prove an agent is safe, and does not cover traffic that doesn't pass through the proxy.
